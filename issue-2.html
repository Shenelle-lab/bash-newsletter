Healthy Crew, Happy Camp

Life at the Mundi Mundi Bash is a little different. For a few weeks each year, crew camp becomes both our workplace and our home.

This newsletter covers everything you need to know about looking after yourself and your fellow crew members, what to pack, and what to expect while living in crew camp.

Whether it's your first Bash or you're a seasoned crewbie, it's worth taking a few minutes to read through before heading to the plains.

Wellbeing & Safety

Looking After Yourself at the Bash

It's more important than ever that crew are happy, engaged and working well together, so here's what you need to know about staying safe, healthy and looking out for one another.

Fitness for Work

This policy outlines the organisation's responsibility to make sure that staff are able to manage their own psychological and physical health, and staff's responsibility to ensure that their behaviour doesn't compromise the safety of anyone else on site.

Staff need to turn up to work clear headed and free of the influence of alcohol and illegal drugs. Here are some ways you can do that.

01

Managing Stress & Fatigue

  • Get plenty of sleep. Pack ear buds and an eye mask if that's what you need.
  • Take scheduled breaks.
  • Recognise if you're too tired, or one of your colleagues is too tired, and let your supervisor know.
  • Let your team leader or a manager know if you have a mental health condition that might affect your ability to manage your job.
02

Managing Your Health & Physical Capacity

Working long hours in remote locations, in physically strenuous jobs and managing competing priorities, can take a toll on your body. We're relying on you to:

  • Be realistic about your physical capacity and decline jobs if you think taking them on would put yourself or others at risk. We can find something else for you to do.
  • Stop work and tell your supervisor if you feel unwell. It's not worth the risk to yourself and others.
  • Bring appropriate clothing (wet weather gear, warm bedding) to help you manage the weather conditions.
  • Bring any medications you might need to stay healthy.
03

Don't Be Under the Influence

  • Don't use alcohol while you're working, and comply with all state and federal laws in relation to illegal drugs. Police are on site, and if we're concerned, we may request that you undertake a blood alcohol or drug test.
  • Heavy drinking while off duty may also mean a raised blood alcohol reading the following day, so be careful. It might also mean you're unable to help if a situation crops up after hours.
  • If it's clear that you're on shift under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs, your employment may be terminated and you may be asked to leave the site.
Team Culture

Bash Mantras, Explained

1

Be Nice

Out here, work and life meld into one for a few weeks a year. That makes it matter even more that crew are happy and working well together.

Our Workplace Harassment, Discrimination and Equal Employment Opportunity Policy sets out how we protect everyone from discrimination based on sex, race, age, disability, sexual orientation and the other characteristics covered under Federal law. Two things it covers:

  • Bullying takes many forms. Jokes, teasing, nicknames, emails, text messages, leaving people out, unfair work practices. The great Aussie put down is a bit on the nose at the Bash. You may think it's funny, and the person may play along to save face, but underneath they're probably feeling like crap. Just don't do it.
  • Sexual harassment is unwelcome sexual behaviour that could be expected to make someone feel offended, humiliated or intimidated. It doesn't matter whether you meant it that way. It can be physical, verbal or written, and one incident is enough. Someone not objecting at the time is not the same as consent.
2

There's Always Time to Work Safely

Strains and sprains are the most common injuries in Australian workplaces, usually from lifting loads that are too heavy. Team up rather than lifting solo, and use machinery when a load is still too heavy. Check with your team leader.

Lifting alone, use the SMART method. Size up the load. Make sure it's close to your body. Always bend your knees. Raise it with your legs. Turn your feet in the direction you want to move.

Same thinking around forklifts and machinery. Stay alert, keep eye contact with the operator, never walk behind an operating machine, never startle an operator. Same again for loading vehicles and for speed limits (10km/h on site, 5km/h in shared zones).

No task is urgent enough to justify cutting a safety corner.

3

If You Don't Know, Ask!

Concerns? Report them. Questions? Ask them. If something doesn't look right, or seems like an accident waiting to happen, head to the Event Office and tell a manager. Do whatever it takes to get their attention.

No one expects you to have all the answers, whether it's your first Bash or your tenth. With a crew this diverse there's always someone who can help, so ask for a second opinion. Double checking a trailer hitch or a load takes a minute.

Crew Camp Guide · 2026 Season

Welcome to the Crew Village

It's your home away from home for the days or weeks you're on site working at the Bash. It's where we eat, sleep, clean off the dust and sweat, and hang out and relax after work. You'll find it tucked away over the road and behind the Event Office, on the eastern side of the Umberumberka Pathway.

Accommodation

Where Will I Sleep?

We provide the spot, you provide your set up. Bring anything from a tent to a fully decked out 14 metre bus, and prepare for some serious rig envy along the way.

Whatever you bring, make sure it's stable and solid enough, and pegged down well enough, to withstand the huge winds we sometimes get out there. If you want to stay dry and put your undies on standing up, we do recommend you leave the swag at home.

If you've already requested a desert tent or camp stretcher from Shenelle, you're all set.

Whether you bring your own tent or use one of ours, you'll still need to bring all your own bedding. Make sure it's warm and comfy.

Cold Nights Tip

There's no power or heating and it can get cold out there at night. Our top tip for cold sleepers is to bring a hot water bottle and fill it up at the urn before bed.

Food & Drink

What Will I Eat and Where's My Coffee Fix?

Our fabulous catering team will serve you three meals a day, from the night before your first shift to the morning after your last.

Brekky and dinner is served in the crew village, and at lunchtime, you'll load up a lunchbox with tasty morsels from the lunch table to have later.

We have sent out the catering email, please make sure to update your dietary requirements for our catering crew.

Barista Coffee in the Outback

Make your own at the brew station in the crew camp kitchen. Fresh beans, dairy and plant based milk options. Your first cup of the day is on us. Remember to bring your own reusable cup.

What to Wear

What Is the Dress Code?

Tough but comfy work clothing and layering are the looks this season. It can be very chilly at night, but dramatically warmer when the sun comes out, so be prepared. And make sure you wear closed shoes.

Do bring gumboots. Weather can change without much warning, and trust us, if it rains, you'll want them. A raincoat is a must, and waterproof pants if you have them. A head torch is indispensable to complete the look.
